The Immaculate Conception: Patroness of Pasig Catholic College

Pasig Catholic College honors Our Lady of the Immaculate Conception as its beloved patroness and spiritual guide. When Spanish missionaries established Catholic communities across the region in the 1500s, Pasig became one of the earliest strongholds of faith. Over the centuries, the devotion to the Blessed Virgin Mary grew into a core foundation of the local community. Recognizing this rich spiritual history, the institution adopted the Immaculate Conception as its patroness, drawing inspiration from her humility, purity, and steadfast devotion to divine guidance.

The Founding Years & The De Brouwer Legacy

The story of Pasig Catholic College traces back to the early 1900s through the dedication of the Congregation of the Immaculate Heart of Mary (CICM). Driven by a mission to provide quality Catholic education, Rev. Fr. Pierre Cornelis De Brouwer, CICM, established the institution in 1913 as Escuela Catolica. The early school aimed to nurture both the academic growth and moral character of young learners in Pasig.

By 1916, during the tenure of Rev. Fr. Paul Hobaux, CICM, the school received formal recognition from the government for its primary curriculum. Expansion continued under Rev. Fr. Godfried Aldenhuijsen, CICM, leading to the approval of intermediate levels in 1920. As student enrollment steadily increased, Rev. Fr. Victor De Klerck, CICM, spearheaded the construction of Bahay Paaralan in 1931—a two-story building featuring eight classrooms near the parish church patio. Between 1939 and 1941, the Urbano Building was erected under the leadership of Rev. Fr. Urbain (Urbano) Timmermans, CICM.

Formal Incorporation & Post-War Expansion

Pasig Catholic College was officially incorporated under Philippine law on August 24, 1915. Following the devastation of World War II, the institution reorganized its corporate structure, filing amended Articles of Incorporation on August 12, 1948, which were formally approved by the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) on February 3, 1949.

A milestone in the school's academic expansion occurred in 1951 with the arrival of Rev. Fr. Roger Bruno Eduard Tjolle, CICM, from Washington, D.C. Under his vision, tertiary programs were introduced, transitioning the institution into a full-fledged college offering secondary and higher education courses to serve the growing community of Pasig and neighboring areas.
